# Ophir Founder Memo

## 1. Why Ophir Exists

The world's financial infrastructure was built for a world of banks. The next generation will increasingly be built for a world of software.

Capital is becoming programmable. Money is becoming digital. Financial assets are becoming tokenized. AI systems are becoming capable of autonomous economic action.

Yet corporate treasury remains fragmented across banking portals, spreadsheets, ERP systems, manual approvals and disconnected financial products.

We believe treasury will become software. Eventually, significant parts of treasury will become autonomous.

**Ophir exists to build the operating system for that transition.**

## 2. Core Thesis

Three structural changes are converging.

### Digital capital

Cash, deposits, funds, bonds, equities, invoices and other financial claims are becoming increasingly digital, programmable and potentially tokenized.

### Artificial intelligence

AI will move from analysis toward action. Corporate agents will forecast cash, reconcile transactions, propose allocations, manage workflows and eventually execute tightly governed financial actions.

### API-native finance

Banks, accounting platforms, ERPs, payment providers and blockchain networks are becoming machine-accessible infrastructure.

The convergence creates a new category: **programmable corporate treasury**.

## 3. The Opportunity

Every meaningful company manages capital, but sophisticated treasury software has historically been concentrated among large enterprises.

Ophir starts with a simpler proposition: give smaller and mid-sized European companies a single intelligent control layer over their financial position.

The long-term market is not "crypto companies." It is companies that manage capital.

## 4. The Problem

A finance team may simultaneously work with several banks, an ERP, accounting software, spreadsheets, payment platforms, investment products and manual approval processes.

This creates poor visibility, duplicated work, slow reconciliation, weak forecasting and limited automation.

Adding stablecoins and tokenized assets without solving this coordination problem would make treasury more complicated, not less.

Ophir should hide that complexity.

## 5. Product Vision

Ophir is not intended to begin as a bank, exchange, custodian or investment adviser.

It begins as the **coordination, intelligence and automation layer** between existing financial infrastructure.

### Phase I — Observe

- Consolidated bank balances
- Wallet and digital-cash visibility
- Treasury dashboard
- Transaction normalization
- Cash-position reporting
- Basic forecasting
- Accounting/ERP exports
- AI treasury copilot

### Phase II — Understand

- Liquidity forecasting
- Risk monitoring
- Reconciliation automation
- Treasury policies
- Scenario analysis
- Tokenized financial-product visibility
- Approval workflows

### Phase III — Orchestrate

- Policy-driven treasury workflows
- Multi-party approvals
- Regulated execution partners
- Automated movement between approved accounts and products
- Agent-assisted treasury operations

### Phase IV — Autonomous Treasury

Within explicit corporate policies, permissions and regulatory boundaries, software agents can continuously optimize routine treasury operations while humans retain governance and exceptional decision-making.

## 6. Strategic Positioning

Ophir is:

- treasury infrastructure, not a crypto application;
- an orchestration layer, not initially a custodian;
- AI-native, but not dependent on AI hype;
- European and compliance-first;
- designed for traditional and tokenized finance simultaneously.

The blockchain should increasingly become invisible to the customer.

## 7. Initial Customer

Start narrow.

The initial hypothesis is a Belgian SME or lower mid-market company with approximately €5M–€100M revenue, multiple financial accounts and enough treasury complexity to feel pain, but not enough scale to justify heavyweight enterprise treasury software.

Belgium is the proving ground. Europe is the intended market.

## 8. Business Model

The core model should be recurring B2B SaaS revenue.

Potential revenue layers:

1. Platform subscription
2. Premium treasury/AI modules
3. Implementation and integrations
4. Enterprise/API access
5. Partner marketplace revenue where legally and strategically appropriate

Ophir should avoid becoming dependent on speculative trading volume.

## 9. Moat

AI models commoditize. Blockchains commoditize. Dashboards can be copied.

The durable moat should emerge from:

- deep bank integrations;
- ERP/accounting integrations;
- normalized financial data;
- treasury workflow history;
- policy and permission infrastructure;
- trusted compliance architecture;
- customer-specific operational context;
- increasingly capable treasury automation built on those layers.

The integration graph becomes the product.

## 10. Regulatory Philosophy

Regulation is an architectural constraint, not an afterthought.

The initial product should deliberately minimize regulated activities where possible: no custody of customer private keys, no holding customer funds, no proprietary exchange, and no unlicensed investment advice or execution.

Exact Belgian and EU regulatory boundaries must be validated with specialist counsel before regulated functionality is shipped.

Where regulated capabilities become strategically necessary, Ophir should first consider integrating licensed partners rather than automatically becoming every regulated entity itself.

## 11. Company Model

Ophir should test whether a radically lean company can build enterprise-grade financial software using AI as organizational leverage.

Principles:

- founder-led;
- AI-native development;
- automated testing and operations;
- automated documentation;
- high software leverage;
- minimal bureaucracy;
- humans hired only where judgment, trust, relationships, domain expertise or accountability genuinely require them.

The ambition is not literally to prohibit hiring. The ambition is to maximize **revenue, product quality and customer value per human**.

## 12. Design Principles

Ophir should be:

- simple;
- trustworthy;
- fast;
- auditable;
- secure;
- API-first;
- policy-driven;
- enterprise-grade;
- comprehensible to a CFO who has no interest in crypto.

## 13. Long-Term Vision

The end state is not a dashboard containing some bank accounts and stablecoins.

The end state is a programmable financial control plane for the enterprise.

Humans define objectives, risk limits, permissions and governance. Ophir observes the company's capital continuously and helps execute routine treasury operations within those boundaries.

## Mission

> **Build the operating system for digital capital.**
