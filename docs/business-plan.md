# Ophir Business Plan

> **Status:** Working document — v0.1
>
> This document is intentionally evidence-driven. Market sizes, regulatory conclusions, pricing and financial projections should not be treated as facts until validated and sourced.

## 1. Executive Summary

To be completed after the underlying analysis.

Core proposition: **Ophir is building the Treasury Operating System for the Tokenized Economy — an AI-native control layer connecting corporate bank accounts, accounting/ERP systems, digital cash and eventually tokenized financial assets.**

## 2. Why Now: The Future of Treasury

Research required:

- adoption of stablecoins and tokenized deposits;
- growth of tokenized money-market funds and bonds;
- European instant-payment infrastructure;
- open-banking/open-finance developments;
- AI agents and machine-to-machine payments;
- regulatory maturation under MiCA and adjacent EU frameworks.

Key question: **What changed in 2024–2026 that makes Ophir possible now but not five years ago?**

## 3. Customer Problem

Validate through interviews rather than assumption.

Hypothesized pain points:

- fragmented bank visibility;
- spreadsheet-driven cash management;
- manual reconciliation;
- poor short-term cash forecasting;
- fragmented approval workflows;
- weak integration between treasury and accounting/ERP;
- complexity introduced by new digital financial rails.

Deliverable: minimum 20 structured CFO/finance-lead interviews before locking the MVP.

## 4. The Ophir Thesis

1. More corporate financial assets become digitally native or tokenized.
2. Corporate finance becomes increasingly API-accessible.
3. AI moves from financial analysis toward governed financial action.
4. Companies need one control plane across traditional and digital rails.
5. Customers care about liquidity, control, risk and automation — not blockchain terminology.

Each thesis will be assigned evidence, counter-evidence and measurable falsification criteria.

## 5. Product

### MVP

Candidate scope:

- bank account aggregation;
- treasury dashboard;
- normalized transaction ledger;
- cash-position reporting;
- cash-flow forecasting;
- read-only wallet/stablecoin visibility;
- accounting/ERP export;
- AI assistant over treasury data;
- audit log and role-based access.

### Explicit MVP exclusions

- custody;
- holding customer money;
- exchange operation;
- discretionary portfolio management;
- autonomous execution of investments;
- unsupported investment recommendations.

### Product evolution

Observe → Understand → Orchestrate → Automate.

## 6. Customer Segmentation

### Beachhead hypothesis

Belgian SMEs/lower mid-market companies:

- €5M–€100M revenue;
- multiple bank accounts and/or legal entities;
- meaningful working-capital complexity;
- finance team without a sophisticated treasury-management system.

Research must determine whether revenue, cash balance, number of bank accounts, entity count or transaction volume is the better segmentation variable.

### Expansion

Belgium → Benelux → EU mid-market → selected enterprise/global use cases.

## 7. Market Size — TAM / SAM / SOM

Do not use inflated top-down fintech TAM numbers.

Build both:

### Bottom-up model

Number of addressable companies × realistic annual contract value.

### Top-down triangulation

Treasury-management software, corporate payments, open-finance and tokenized-asset infrastructure markets.

Required scenarios: conservative / base / upside.

## 8. Competitive Landscape

Analyze categories rather than only direct competitors:

- enterprise treasury-management systems;
- SME cash-management platforms;
- ERP/accounting suites;
- banks and bank portals;
- payment infrastructure;
- stablecoin infrastructure;
- institutional digital-asset infrastructure;
- custody platforms;
- AI finance tools.

For every relevant competitor document:

- target customer;
- core product;
- pricing where available;
- regulated status;
- integrations;
- strengths;
- weaknesses;
- overlap with Ophir;
- credible response if Ophir succeeds.

## 9. Differentiation & Moat

Candidate moat layers:

1. Integration graph
2. Normalized treasury data model
3. Policy/permissions engine
4. Workflow history
5. Customer-specific financial context
6. Compliance architecture
7. Distribution partnerships
8. Trust and security reputation

Critical question: **Why can SAP, Kyriba, a bank, Stripe, Fireblocks or a future AI-native entrant not simply copy Ophir?**

## 11. Business Model & Pricing

Test rather than assume.

Potential structure:

- entry SaaS tier;
- professional multi-entity tier;
- enterprise tier;
- implementation/integration fees;
- premium automation and AI modules;
- API pricing.

Pricing should correlate with customer value and treasury complexity rather than token or transaction speculation.

## 12. Regulatory Strategy — Belgium & EU

Requires dedicated legal research and specialist validation.

Map functionality against at least:

- MiCA/CASP requirements;
- PSD2 and evolving EU payments framework;
- MiFID II where financial instruments are involved;
- AML/KYC obligations;
- DORA applicability and vendor expectations;
- GDPR;
- Belgian FSMA/NBB responsibilities;
- rules governing tokenized securities/funds and financial promotion/advice.

For each feature classify:

**Green:** software/data activity likely outside licensing perimeter.

**Amber:** requires legal analysis and/or licensed partner.

**Red:** regulated activity Ophir deliberately avoids initially.

This regulatory perimeter will directly influence product architecture.

## 13. Technical Architecture

Design requirements:

- multi-tenant SaaS;
- secure bank connectors;
- chain abstraction;
- double-entry/normalized internal ledger;
- immutable audit trail;
- granular RBAC;
- policy engine;
- ERP/accounting connectors;
- AI tool permissioning;
- human approval boundaries;
- observability;
- EU-oriented data governance;
- security suitable for enterprise finance.

## 14. AI-Native Operating Model

Ophir aims to maximize output per human through AI-assisted:

- software development;
- testing;
- research;
- documentation;
- support;
- sales preparation;
- customer onboarding;
- internal operations.

AI should reduce headcount requirements, not weaken security, review or accountability.

Track **ARR per employee** and **customers per employee** as strategic operating metrics.

## 15. Roadmap

### Stage 0 — Validation

Customer interviews, regulatory mapping, competitor analysis and prototype.

### Stage 1 — Read-only MVP

Aggregate and understand treasury data.

### Stage 2 — Workflow system

Approvals, reconciliation, policies and deeper integrations.

### Stage 3 — Regulated-partner orchestration

Enable approved financial actions through licensed infrastructure partners.

### Stage 4 — Governed agents

Increasingly autonomous routine treasury actions within explicit policy boundaries.

## 16. Financial Model

Model:

- ACV;
- implementation revenue;
- gross margin;
- infrastructure cost;
- sales cost;
- headcount;
- legal/compliance cost;
- security cost;
- burn;
- runway;
- ARR;
- cash-flow break-even.

Use conservative, base and upside scenarios.

## 17. Risks & Falsification

Major risks:

- tokenization adoption is slower than expected;
- SMEs do not care enough about treasury optimization;
- incumbent TMS vendors move downmarket;
- banks bundle comparable functionality;
- integration burden destroys one-person economics;
- regulatory perimeter becomes too expensive;
- customers refuse AI access to financial workflows;
- security requirements demand substantially larger teams;
- stablecoin/tokenized-asset adoption fragments across incompatible rails.

The plan should explicitly define conditions under which Ophir pivots or stops.

## 18. Long-Term Vision

Ophir starts by showing companies where their capital is.

Then it helps them understand it.

Then it helps them coordinate it.

Eventually it helps them operate it autonomously within human-defined policies.

**The long-term product is a financial control plane for the enterprise.**
